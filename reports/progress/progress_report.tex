\section{The problem and the methods (unchanged)}
Shenzhen had 1.08 million new-energy vehicles by June 2024~\cite{cnev2024}, and the national NEV stock grew by 49\% in the following year~\cite{cnev2025}. We study whether the city's \textbf{17{,}532 public charging piles in 275 traffic zones} (UrbanEV dataset~\cite{li2025urbanev}, hourly, Sep 2022--Feb 2023) can absorb this growth, and how to fix it at least cost. Average occupancy is only 28\%, yet a few zones saturate, mostly at night: the network suffers from a \emph{spatial and temporal mismatch} rather than a simple shortage. Decision problem: keep driver waiting time low under demand growth $g\in\{1.5,2,2.5\}$ using four levers: new piles, relocation of existing piles, navigation guidance and a time-of-use service fee.

\begin{tabular}{@{}p{3.1cm}p{5.4cm}p{7.1cm}@{}}\toprule
Method & What it answers & Course topic and why\\\midrule
M1 Queueing (M/M/$c$) & waiting per zone and hour; which zones fail & queueing theory; Erlang-C links piles, load and delay\\
M2 Integer programming & where to add and where to remove piles under a budget & LP/IP; indivisible piles, budget couples all zones; LP duality gives the budget's shadow price\\
M3 Network flow & how much overload guidance can absorb within a detour limit & network optimisation; transportation problem, integer flows by total unimodularity\\
M4 Nonlinear programming & which hourly fee shifts demand away from congested hours & NLP and KKT conditions; logit demand response, revenue neutrality\\
M5 Simulation + simulated annealing & do M1/M2 hold under time-varying demand and spill-over? & simulation and metaheuristics; noisy, non-separable objective\\\bottomrule
